Scope: what Michael Gerber's materials say about business management areas, measures, documents, roles, and routines, mapped to a COD e-commerce admin app for a solo Nigerian founder selling in Ghana (hiring staff and adding AI agents soon).

## 1. The framework in plain language

Gerber's core idea: a business is a system that works without the owner. The owner's job is to build that system, measure it, and staff it with people who follow written standards. Three activities repeat: **Innovation** (do new things), **Quantification** (measure them), **Orchestration** (make the result repeat) [REV p.92-98, CONFIRMED].

The Business Development Program has seven steps [REV p.105, CONFIRMED]:

| Step | Plain meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Aim | What the owner wants from life. The business serves that. | REV p.106-109 |
| Strategic Objective | A written statement of what the business must do for the owner. First standard is money. Second is an "opportunity worth pursuing" (it fixes a frustration for enough buyers). | REV p.116-121 |
| Organizational Strategy | Org chart and a Position Contract for every box. | REV p.128-140 |
| Management Strategy | How managers run the business and make decisions. | REV p.143-152 (read partly) |
| People Strategy | How people are recruited, trained, and kept in the system (Operations Manuals, first-day training). | REV p.153-163 |
| Marketing Strategy | Know the customer (demographics, psychographics). Build positioning. | REV p.165-175; WB MK-0010, MK-0030, MK-0040 |
| Systems Strategy | Every process is a system; information systems tie them together. | REV p.177-188 |

Gerber's business functions (REV p.174-175, CONFIRMED): **Marketing = Lead Generation; Sales = Lead Conversion; Operations = Client Fulfillment**. Each Vice-President owns one, and the COO keeps the whole consistent. Finance supports all three. The workbook uses the same three-part model: lead generation, lead conversion, client fulfillment (WB chapter folders 5, 6, 7).

Gerber's four-way test of any business (ENT p.33, CONFIRMED): the business must satisfy its four "primary influencers" (employees, customers, suppliers, lenders). ENT also names four categories of preference (visual, emotional, functional, financial ideals; ENT p.76, CONFIRMED). These are a different lens from the REV functions. Do not mix them up.

Key owner decisions (from the workbook Business Plan checklist, WB chapter1 "Business Plan.pdf" p.2, CONFIRMED): what the business is for; the time horizon; who runs each area; which numbers are tracked and by whom; which assumptions are monitored and when they are reviewed (WB chapter1 "Business Plan.pdf" p.4).

## 2. Each area: meaning, measures, documents, routines

Each block: owner decision/tracking, KPIs Gerber names or implies, documents/systems, routines. Gerber names few numeric targets. Most KPIs below are "what to count," not targets. Targets are GUESS unless marked.

### 2.1 Strategic Objective and money (Finance strategy)

- **Decision:** how much cash the owner takes out vs. reinvests. Trade-off between current income and business value [WB chapter3 "Financial Strategies.pdf" p.1-3, CONFIRMED]. 
- **Money in / money out** [WB "Financial Strategies.pdf" p.1, CONFIRMED]: In = owner investment, operating revenue, borrowing, value added by systems. Out = owner payouts, operating expenses, debt service, "wild cards" (disasters, risk, litigation).
- **Measures named:** price per unit, units sold, variable cost per unit, fixed expenses, contribution, profit, break-even revenue (= fixed expenses / contribution %) and break-even units (= break-even revenue / price) [WB "Financial Strategies.pdf" p.1-2, CONFIRMED formulas].
- **Cash measures** [WB "Maximum Cash.pdf" p.1-7, CONFIRMED]:
  - Average daily deposits minus average daily payments minus cash reserve = excess balance.
  - Days receivable (current, 30-59, 60-89, 90+ past due buckets). Cash released = days reduced x dollars per day.
  - Days of inventory; cash released per day of inventory.
  - Days of payables; cash released per day gained.
  - Monthly cash expenses; monthly cash paid to suppliers.
  - Six rules for cash power: decrease assets, increase liabilities or capital, increase revenue, decrease cash expenses, improve productivity, optimize timing [WB "Maximum Cash.pdf" p.2, CONFIRMED].
- **Documents:** Financial Strategies Worksheet (short- and long-term approach for owner investment, revenue, borrowing, systems value, expenses, taxes, wild cards) [WB "Financial Strategies.pdf" p.4-6, CONFIRMED]. Pricing grid (baseline plus "what-if" with stated assumptions) [WB p.1-3, CONFIRMED].
- **Routines:** Bank balances and reserves reviewed (Cash Power Checklist asks "Do you monitor your bank accounts daily?") [WB "Maximum Cash.pdf" p.4, CONFIRMED]. Assumptions reviewed on a set date [WB "Business Plan.pdf" p.4, CONFIRMED].
- **Money integrity (ENT):** "The primary financial objective of your business is to maximize its financial value" [WB "Financial Strategies.pdf" p.3, CONFIRMED]. ENT p.172 (CONFIRMED): bankers, accountants, suppliers, customers, and employees must see integrity with money. "You back up your word with compatible actions."

### 2.2 Lead generation (Marketing)

- **Decision:** which channels, which message, what budget per channel.
- **Measures named or implied:**
  - Cost of one placement; reach of one placement; cost per person reached = cost / reach; coverage = reach / target population [WB "Lead Generation Channels.pdf" p.2, CONFIRMED].
  - Subjective ranking: fit with target market, fit with company image, ability to use the channel, impact, credibility, intimacy [WB "Lead Generation Channels.pdf" p.3, CONFIRMED].
  - Budget: development cost + (cost per placement x placements per year) = total needed; compare to money allocated [WB "Lead Generation Channels.pdf" p.5, CONFIRMED].
  - REV p.95-96 (CONFIRMED): quantify "everything." Count people who come in or call per day, how many ask for price, how many buy, what sells, when. Example claim: "increase sales 10 percent" by a small change. Gerber's "99 percent" anecdote: most owners cannot say how many selling opportunities they had yesterday (REV p.95).
- **Documents:** Lead Generation Checklist and Selection List [WB "Lead Generation Channels.pdf" p.1, p.4, CONFIRMED]. Ten-step lead piece worksheet: desired result, context, instant of connection, logic, content, qualifiers, brand, action step [WB "Application of Lead Generation Principles.pdf" p.1-5, CONFIRMED]. Controlling Calendar: item, channel, cost, accountability, timing [WB "Lead Generation Channels.pdf" p.6, CONFIRMED].
- **Who the message is for:** Central Demographic Model and Psychographic Model: age, income, location, values, gratification mode, purchase preference [WB "Your Most Probable Customer.pdf" p.2, CONFIRMED; WB "Customer Perceptions and Behavior.pdf" p.1-4, CONFIRMED].
- **Routines:** A controlling calendar per channel. Lead-piece qualifiers screen people in or out. Gerber: "anyone can respond who wants to respond, qualified or not" [WB "Application..." p.2, CONFIRMED].

### 2.3 Lead conversion (Sales)

- **Decision:** what a prospect needs to say yes.
- **Measures:** the workbook frames conversion by need, not by a number. Purchase Decision Needs = Impact (high/med/low) x Confidence (high/med/low) [WB "Your Lead Conversion Process.pdf" p.1, CONFIRMED]. Selling functions: information, understanding, needs clarification, advice, assistance, reassurance, transaction processing [WB p.1-2, CONFIRMED]. Gerber names no conversion-rate target in these pages (GAP).
- **Documents:** Sales Operations Manual with exact scripts for incoming calls, outgoing calls, and meeting the customer [REV p.138, CONFIRMED]. Scripts were written only after the innovation was quantified. Lead Conversion Process worksheet asks for a box-and-arrow diagram naming "the universal five steps of lead conversion" [WB LC-0020 p.3, CONFIRMED]. The five steps are NOT named in the readable text (GAP).
- **Routines:** Not specified in these pages. REV p.138 shows sales innovations measured before they go into the manual (CONFIRMED). A weekly review cadence is GUESS.

### 2.4 Client fulfillment (Operations / delivery)

- **Decision:** how product moves from business to customer, and what extras (service) are offered.
- **Three processes** [WB "Your Client Fulfillment Baseline" p.4, CONFIRMED]: production, delivery, customer service. Delivery types: in-business (product made at your site, shipped) and on-site [WB p.2, CONFIRMED]. Customer service = anything beyond what the customer must have to buy or use the product [WB p.2, CONFIRMED].
- **Measures:** Process Baseline worksheet: inputs, process, outputs, cost. Each input and output is tracked on quantity, quality, timing, intangibles [WB "Your Client Fulfillment Baseline" p.2, CONFIRMED]. Key Indicators worksheet: baseline, previous period, current period, per process [WB p.3-4, CONFIRMED].
- **Systems innovation checklist** (use on each process) [WB "Systems Innovation.pdf" p.1-3, CONFIRMED]: output (waste and rework at minimum), benchmarks, sequence (run steps in parallel to cut cycle time), internal and external balance (inputs at right rate and quality), spin-offs and mergers, consolidation, staff distribution, supplies on hand when needed, information easy to read, layout, inspection points, idle points, equipment, centralized vs decentralized, management decision points that stall work, and gathering management info by piggybacking on existing documents.
- **Innovation process** (9 steps): select a system, observe it on site, diagram it, analyze work flow, set a baseline, apply the checklist, diagram the new version, estimate performance vs. baseline, install and test [WB "Systems Innovation.pdf" p.2, CONFIRMED].
- **Cash in fulfilment** [WB "Maximum Cash.pdf" p.5, CONFIRMED]: outsourcing decision = one-time cash inflow + monthly operating savings. Also ask whether non-hire options exist before hiring.

### 2.5 Client re-conversion (repeat business)

- Gerber's customer dynamics: re-conversion does not add customers. It slows attrition and raises revenue by increasing frequency and average sale [WB "Client Re-Conversion.pdf" p.2, CONFIRMED].
- **Measures implied:** points of access (where customer data is captured) in lead generation, conversion, delivery, service. For each: information needed, procedure, instruments [WB "Client Re-Conversion.pdf" p.1, p.2, CONFIRMED].
- **Documents:** Customer Information Collection Plan; Re-Conversion Program Outline (segment, activities, how each adds value); Customer Communication Worksheet (activity, channel and frequency, message, comments) [WB "Client Re-Conversion.pdf" p.1-5, CONFIRMED].
- **Customer demographics questionnaire** records purchase yes/no, amount, item, and date [WB "Your Most Probable Customer.pdf" p.3, CONFIRMED].

### 2.6 Management, people, and culture

- **Decision:** who is accountable for which system, and what the managers do each week.
- **Management by walking around** (Hewlett-Packard, MBWA): regular visits and talk with staff about work [WB "Creating a High-Performance Environment.pdf" p.1, CONFIRMED]. Also: formal meetings, informal "coffee hours" [same, p.1].
- **Vision, structure, and tools** make a high-performance environment. Money is not required [WB "Creating a High-Performance Environment.pdf" p.3, CONFIRMED].
- **Training and tools:** "how to do it" (training) and "how to get it done" (tools): information, organizational skills, self-knowledge skills [WB "Creating a High-Performance Environment.pdf" p.2, CONFIRMED].
- **Productivity:** ongoing measurement of employee productivity; when it is weak, retrain, replace, or fix the system [WB "Maximum Cash.pdf" p.7, CONFIRMED].
- **Staffing:** keep permanent staff for base workload; use overtime or temps for peaks. Measure seasonality [WB "Maximum Cash.pdf" p.7, CONFIRMED].

## 3. Quantification: what Gerber says to count

Three levels [WB "Quantification.pdf" p.1-2, CONFIRMED]:

1. **Key Strategic Indicators.** Progress toward the Strategic Objective and overall health. Review at least quarterly. Not precise enough to diagnose problems.
2. **Key Business Indicators.** Let the CEO and senior managers run the business as one whole. Worksheet fields: description and units; source and timing (daily, hourly, weekly); accountable person; priority; management report name [WB "Quantification.pdf" p.1, CONFIRMED].
3. **Key System Indicators.** Every system gets indicators and a baseline when it is created or changed. Staff watch them continuously. These show what is wrong and where [WB "Quantification.pdf" p.1, CONFIRMED].

Management reports: each report has a name and timing, description and units, accountable person, and distribution list [WB "Quantification.pdf" p.2, CONFIRMED]. Example report set: production tracking, staffing, customer tracking, sales/response, advertising, administration/overhead analysis [WB "Quantification.pdf" p.3, CONFIRMED].

Gerber's standing examples of what to count (REV p.96, CONFIRMED): customers seen per day (morning and afternoon), callers per day, callers asking for a price, buyers, units per product per day, time of day sold, weekly totals, busiest days.

Assumption tracking [WB "Business Plan.pdf" p.4, CONFIRMED]: each planning assumption, the plan parts it affects, the indicators to watch, the review date, and whether a change is needed.

Numeric targets: Gerber gives none for conversion, fulfilment, or lead costs in these sources. Only the example "sell the business for more than $1 million in seven years" appears (REV p.125, an anecdote). GAP.

## 4. Org chart, roles, and position contracts

Gerber's pre-hire sequence [REV p.128-140, CONFIRMED]:

1. Write the Strategic Objective first. The org chart flows down from it (REV p.139).
2. Draw the org chart for the business as it will be in the future, with every box needed for full potential, not the current headcount (REV p.133; REV p.140: "an Organization Chart for ... as it will look when it's done, seven years from now").
3. Write a **Position Contract** for each box. Contents: results to be achieved, the work the occupant is accountable for, standards for evaluation, and a signature line (REV p.133-134, CONFIRMED; wording partly cut off in extraction).
   - Gerber: "Accountability literally means 'stand up and be counted'" (REV p.134).
4. Owner signs each contract. When the owner is doing a job, he signs as that job too (REV p.135-136, p.140).
5. Name the people last (REV p.134).
6. Build the Operations Manual for each position before hiring (REV p.138: "Only when the Sales Operations Manual is complete does Murray run an ad for a salesperson").
7. Hiring: show the org chart, the manual, and the Strategic Objective. Show where the position reports and who is accountable now (REV p.138-139).
8. First-day training: review the owner's idea, tour the facilities, give uniform and Operations Manual, review the contract, do employment papers (REV p.159, CONFIRMED).

Example chart from REV p.133 (CONFIRMED):
- President/COO
  - VP Marketing: Sales Manager; Advertising/Research Manager
  - VP Operations: Production Manager; Service Manager; Facilities Manager
  - VP Finance: Accounts Receivable Manager; Accounts Payable Manager

The chart in REV is for a manufacturer. It maps to a COD business by swapping the boxes (see Section 5).

Operations manual index [WB "Operations Manuals.pdf" p.1-2, CONFIRMED]:
- Company documents: Strategic Objective, company story, organization chart, company history.
- Products and services: positioning statement and USP, brochure, product education.
- Policies: employee handbook, benefits.
- Per position: position contracts (own and reports'), and every system the position is accountable for.

Solo founder today. Gerber's method for a one-person firm: the owner fills every box and signs each contract as the employee (REV p.140, CONFIRMED). Hiring order is GUESS: write the contract and manual first, then hire (REV p.138).

## 6. Gaps

- **Enterprise book functions.** The user's prompt listed "leadership, marketing, finance, management" and "lead generation, lead conversion, client fulfillment" as ENT functions. ENT does not use those function names in readable text. The three-part model is in REV p.174-175 and in the workbook. ENT uses four categories of preference (visual, emotional, functional, financial) and four primary influencers (ENT p.33, p.76).
- **ENT fifth essential skill.** Only four skills are readable: concentration, discrimination, organization, innovation (ENT p.60-61). The fifth was not found.
- **ENT text quality.** Many ENT pages are garbled or blank in the text layer (e.g. pp.9-10, 29). I used pdftotext for both books instead of the Read tool, because the books are 206 and 232 pages. Read tool was used for every workbook PDF, including Positioning and Differentiating.
- **REV Management Strategy (ch.15, PDF p.143-152).** Read in part only.
- **Positioning (WB MK-0040) is read.** Its USP rules (WB "Positioning and Differentiating.pdf" p.8-10) are not used in the table.
- **Lead conversion five steps.** Named in WB LC-0020 p.3 but not in readable text. Not found.
- **Numeric KPI targets.** Not in these sources, apart from break-even and cash formulas.
- **ENT chapters 4-6 (PDF p.51-160).** Only short takeaway pages were read.
- **Customer-service KPIs, returns, fraud, COD remittance, 3PL metrics:** not covered by Gerber. Everything in those rows is GUESS.
